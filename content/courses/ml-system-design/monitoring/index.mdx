Monitoring has two halves: **what you watch**, and **what you do when something breaks**.

## What you watch: a stack of layers

Problems usually start low in the stack and show up higher. The top three layers are ML-specific; normal service monitoring doesn't cover them.

<Diagram src="monitoring/monitoring-layers-stack.svg" />

| Layer | Question | Signals |
|---|---|---|
| Business KPIs | Is it doing what we built it for? | CTR, revenue, retention |
| Model quality | Is it still accurate? (needs ground truth) | Accuracy, calibration, slices |
| Drift | Has the distribution shifted? | Data drift, prediction drift, concept drift |
| Data quality | Are inputs sane? | Schema, nulls, ranges |
| System health | Is the service up? (classic SRE) | Latency, throughput, errors |

## What you do: the response loop

<Diagram src="monitoring/response-loop.svg" />

**Detect** (alert on a threshold) → **Diagnose** (which layer broke?) → **Fix a data bug**, **retrain** on fresh data, or **roll back** to the last good model → **Validate** (offline + shadow) → **Canary deploy** → back to monitoring.

Start diagnosis at the bottom of the stack. Most real incidents are data bugs, not model decay.

## Retraining strategies

| Strategy | When | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduled (daily/weekly) | Slow, predictable drift | Simple, but may retrain for nothing or react too late |
| Trigger-based | A drift or performance alert fires | Efficient, but needs reliable detection |
| Continual / online | Very fast change (ads, feeds) | Fresh, but complex and risky (feedback loops) |

## Running example: PayCo

PayCo scores card transactions for fraud with a gradient-boosted model using velocity and geo features, then blocks or allows. Truth arrives as chargebacks around day 60. Meanwhile the service dashboard shows 200 OK, p99 38 ms, 0 errors.
