## 5.1 Label delay

### The problem it solves
Every quality metric needs the **true outcome**. In most real systems the truth arrives **late**:

| Domain | Label | Typical delay |
|---|---|---|
| Ads | Click | Seconds |
| Recommendations | Watch / skip | Minutes |
| E-commerce | Purchase / return | Days to weeks |
| **Card fraud (PayCo)** | **Chargeback** | **Days to 120 days** |
| Lending | Default | Months to years |
| Medical | Outcome | Years |

During that gap, you're **flying blind on quality**. Worse, naive measurements made during the gap are **systematically wrong**, and wrong in a way that looks good.

### How it works: the maturity curve
Labels don't all arrive at the same age. Plot the **cumulative share of eventual chargebacks that have arrived**, by days since the transaction:

### The trap: immature cohorts always look better
True fraud rate = 0.10% in every week. What a naive dashboard shows today:

| Cohort (transaction week) | Age | Maturity | Observed fraud rate | Looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12 weeks ago | 84 d | ~99% | 0.099% | Normal |
| 8 weeks ago | 56 d | ~94% | 0.094% | Normal |
| 4 weeks ago | 28 d | ~78% | 0.078% | "Improving" |
| 2 weeks ago | 14 d | 55% | 0.055% | "Big improvement!" |
| Last week | 7 d | 30% | 0.030% | "Fraud down 70%!" |

**Recent cohorts always look healthier**, and the model deployed most recently always looks like a hero. It's the most common false conclusion in delayed-label systems.

### Three techniques to deal with it

**1. Cohort by prediction time (from 1.3).**
Group by **when the transaction happened**, never by when the label arrived.

**2. Compare at equal age (fixed-horizon metrics).**
Define **"14-day fraud rate"** = chargebacks arriving within 14 days of the transaction. Compare the new model's cohorts with the old model's cohorts **at the same age**. It's a fair comparison available at day 14, as long as the maturity curve is stable.

**3. Maturity adjustment (the actuarial approach).**
Insurance has the same problem: claims that are **incurred but not reported** (IBNR). Their fix works here:
```
estimated_final_rate(cohort) = observed_rate(age) / maturity(age)
```
Last week: 0.030% / 0.30 = **0.10%**. The trend disappears. You can also attach uncertainty, since young cohorts are noisier. Estimate maturity curves **per segment**: online vs. card-present and domestic vs. international mature at different speeds.

(More formally, it's **survival analysis**: time-to-label with right-censoring. The maturity curve is 1 − the survival function.)

### Label delay also affects training
If you retrain on data up to yesterday, recent frauds **haven't been labeled yet**, so they enter training as **legitimate**. That teaches the model that recent fraud patterns are safe, which is precisely the concept drift you're trying to learn.

Options:
- **Exclude immature data**: train only on transactions older than the horizon (e.g. 90 days). Correct, but always **~3 months stale**.
- **Shorter horizon**: label = "fraud within 30 days" (80% complete). Fresher, with 20% label noise that you accept knowingly.
- **Maturity weighting**: include recent data, but down-weight negatives by the probability they'll still turn fraud.
- **Two-stage**: the main model on mature data plus a fast-adapting component on proxies (5.2).

### Assumptions
1. **The maturity curve is stable.** It isn't always. A holiday backlog at issuers, a card-network rule change on dispute windows, or a new issuer with slow processes all change it, and every adjustment becomes wrong.
2. **The label horizon captures nearly everything.** Late chargebacks beyond 90 days are treated as legitimate.
3. **Every transaction can eventually get a label.** **Blocked transactions never do.** That's 5.3.

### Trade-offs
- **Wait vs. act**: waiting for maturity gives the truth too late to prevent losses. Acting on early reads is fast but depends on the maturity model.
- **Horizon length**: long = complete labels, stale model. Short = fresh model, noisy labels.
- **Per-segment curves vs. data**: more accurate, but small segments don't have enough chargebacks to estimate their own curve. Pool or use hierarchical estimates.

### Failure modes
- **"Fraud dropped 70% after launch!"** from comparing immature cohorts with mature ones.
- **Label-arrival windows** instead of cohorts → metrics mixing transactions from months apart.
- **Training on immature labels** → the model systematically underestimates recent fraud patterns.
- **Stale maturity curve** → all adjustments are silently off. You need to **monitor the maturity curve itself** by comparing each new cohort's arrival pace with the historical curve.
- **Forgetting censored (blocked) transactions** → covered in 5.3.

## 5.2 Proxies

Use early signals (e.g. analyst review outcomes, disputes) as proxies until real labels arrive.

## 5.3 Feedback

The model's actions hide some labels. Keep a small exploration sample to measure what it blocks.
