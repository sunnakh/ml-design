Evaluation is a two-gate funnel. Offline checks are cheap, fast, and don't touch users. Online checks cost more and carry more risk, but they're the only real proof.

<Diagram src="evaluation/offline-online-funnel.svg" />

## Offline evaluation (held-out data, no users)

- **Data splits**: split by time whenever the data is time-ordered; split by user for per-user tasks. A random split leaks future information and inflates scores.
- **Metrics**: classification: precision, recall, PR-AUC (better than ROC-AUC when classes are imbalanced), log loss. Ranking: NDCG@k, MAP, recall@k. Regression: RMSE, MAE. Check calibration when probabilities feed decisions.
- **Slice analysis**: break results down by segment (new vs returning users, country, device). The model can win on average and still hurt a group that matters.
- **Error analysis**: read the worst errors by hand and group them.
- **Offline gate**: beat the baseline without regressing on key slices or latency. If it fails, go back to modeling.

## Online evaluation (real users)

- **Shadow mode**: the new model runs on live traffic but isn't served. Catches crashes, latency problems and skew at zero risk.
- **A/B test**: choose the randomization unit, compute sample size first, pick one primary metric plus guardrails, run at least a full week, and watch for novelty and network effects.
- **Interleaving**: for ranking, mix results from A and B into one list and let clicks decide. Needs 10–100× less traffic than an A/B test.

## Running example

An online store's homepage has a **"Recommended for you" row**. Today it shows the **top 20 bestsellers** to everyone. Your team trained a **personalized model** and wants to ship. Your job is to decide whether they should.
