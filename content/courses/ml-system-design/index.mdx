ML system design is about turning a vague business problem into a working ML system that can be trained, served, evaluated and maintained in production. This course follows 8 stages in 3 phases, in the order you would use in a real design review or a 45-minute interview.

<Diagram src="problem-framing/ml-system-design-8-stages.svg" />

## The 8 stages

<Cards>
  <Card icon={<Target />} title="1. Problem Framing" href="/courses/ml-system-design/problem-framing">
    What business problem are we solving? Is ML even needed? What kind of ML task is it?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<ClipboardList />} title="2. Requirements" href="/courses/ml-system-design/requirements">
    How do we measure success? What are the latency, scale and cost limits?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<Database />} title="3. Data" href="/courses/ml-system-design/data">
    Where does the data come from, and how do we get labels?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<Layers />} title="4. Features" href="/courses/ml-system-design/features">
    Which signals predict the target? How are they computed and stored?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<Network />} title="5. Modeling" href="/courses/ml-system-design/modeling">
    What's the baseline? Which model do we pick after that, and how do we train it?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<ChartColumn />} title="6. Evaluation" href="/courses/ml-system-design/evaluation">
    Does the model beat the baseline offline? Does it hold up in an A/B test?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<Rocket />} title="7. Deployment" href="/courses/ml-system-design/deployment">
    Batch or online? How do we meet the latency limit and roll out safely?
  </Card>
  <Card icon={<Activity />} title="8. Monitoring" href="/courses/ml-system-design/monitoring">
    Is the model degrading, or has the data drifted? When should we retrain?
  </Card>
</Cards>

## It's a loop, not a line

Production ML systems never finish. What monitoring finds sends you back to earlier stages: a data bug back to Data, drift back to retraining, a changed world back to Problem Framing. Interviewers look for this.

<Callout title="Interview tip">
  Stages 1–2 are where most people rush, and they carry the most weight in interviews and in real projects.
</Callout>
